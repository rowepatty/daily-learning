HTML & CSS — Try It Today

Flexbox centering

For decades, perfectly centering a box inside another box (both left-right AND up-down) was one of the trickiest things in CSS. Flexbox solves it in just three lines: give the parent 'display: flex; justify-content: center; align-items: center;' and the child lands dead center, no matter its size.

Save as center.html and open it:

<!doctype html>
<html><head><style>
  .box {
    display: flex;
    justify-content: center;  /* centers left-right */
    align-items: center;      /* centers up-down */
    height: 300px;
    background: linear-gradient(135deg, #6dd5ed, #2193b0);
    border-radius: 12px;
  }
  .card {
    background: white;
    padding: 24px 40px;
    border-radius: 8px;
    font-family: sans-serif;
  }
</style></head>
<body>
  <div class="box">
    <div class="card">I'm perfectly centered!</div>
  </div>
</body></html>

Try making the .box taller, or add more text to the .card - it stays centered.

Why it’s cool: It ends the infamous 'how do I center a div?' struggle with three lines you'll reuse forever, and it stays centered automatically even when the content changes size.
